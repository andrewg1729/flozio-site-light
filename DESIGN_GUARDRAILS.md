# Flozio website design and content rules

Use these rules for Flozio's public website. The goal is a clean, familiar SaaS experience that quickly explains the product, shows how it helps, and makes the next step obvious.

## Use proven SaaS patterns

- Use a conventional header with clear product navigation and a visible primary action.
- Use a direct hero: say that Flozio is sales engagement software, explain the practical benefit, and offer a clear next step.
- A copy-and-product-preview hero, benefit sections, feature cards, customer proof, and a closing CTA are all appropriate. These familiar patterns are not a problem when the content is specific and the page is easy to use.
- Keep the page scannable. Give each section one job and use headings that explain the section without requiring a slogan to decode it.
- Keep interaction patterns familiar. Do not make navigation or buttons unusual just to make the design look different.

## Flozio's visual identity

- Keep the site light, clean, and modern.
- Use the supplied Flozio mark and its blue shades as the primary palette: deep navy for text and structure, bright blue for actions and links, and cyan as a supporting accent.
- Do not let teal, purple, or generic gradients take over the brand.
- Use a consistent, legible sans-serif type system. Make hierarchy through size, weight, spacing, and line length rather than ornamental type treatments.
- Keep decoration restrained. Avoid glowing blobs, glass panels, floating badges, pulsing dots, and motion that does not explain the product or help the user.

## Never publish invented or vague content

- Never invent customer names, logos, testimonials, awards, adoption counts, performance metrics, or outcome claims.
- Never present a concept interface as a screenshot of the live Flozio product. Label illustrative product examples as examples.
- Never claim a product feature until Flozio confirms it. Use the confirmed sales engagement category and workflow details; ask when a claim needs verification.
- Never use a vague headline that could fit any SaaS company. State the product category and a practical sales-team benefit in plain language.
- Never use filler phrases such as "revolutionize your workflow," "the future of sales," "everything in one place," or "work smarter" without concrete explanation.
- Never use a CTA that misstates what happens after the click. Demo and contact CTAs must go to the confirmed contact destination: andrew@getflozio.com.
- Never leave visible navigation, buttons, or links without a useful destination.

## Before publishing

1. Can a new visitor tell within a few seconds that Flozio is sales engagement software?
2. Does the headline explain a practical benefit for a sales team?
3. Is the main action clear, consistent, and connected to the confirmed email or demo flow?
4. Are product details and all customer proof real and approved?
5. Is the blue palette recognizable, the type readable, and the page usable on mobile and with a keyboard?

## References

- [ClickUp homepage](https://clickup.com/) is a reference for clear product navigation, a direct value proposition, product-led proof, scannable sections, and a visible conversion action. Use the structure as a model; keep Flozio's wording and identity original.
- [Nielsen Norman Group: Maintain Consistency and Adhere to Standards](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/consistency-and-standards/) explains why familiar interaction patterns aid learnability and reduce confusion.
- [Creative Bloq: Everything looks the same. Now what?](https://www.creativebloq.com/ai/everything-looks-the-same-now-what) discusses visual sameness and how generative tools can amplify existing design defaults.
- [Designpixil: AI Slop: Why Everything Designed With AI Looks the Same](https://designpixil.com/blog/ai-slop-design) gives a practitioner perspective on generic UI, copy, and the value of brand-specific constraints. Treat its observations as editorial guidance, not a formal test for AI authorship.
